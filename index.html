Foodprint Malaysia:
Mapping McDonald's and Malaysia's Fast-Food Culture (2022)

The distribution of McDonald's outlets across Malaysian states reflects urbanisation, population density, and lifestyle patterns. Beyond outlet counts, nutritional data reveals the health side of Malaysia's fast-food culture - showing how diet and lifestyle intersect across regions.

McDonald's Malaysia Corporate Milestones

Population and McDonald's Outlets in Malaysian States (2022)

The 2022 data highlights clear regional disparities in McDonald's outlet distribution across Malaysia. Selangor leads with 97 outlets, followed by Kuala Lumpur (45) and Johor (40), showcasing the concentration of fast-food chains in areas with strong economies, urban growth, and high population density. These regions also serve as commercial and logistic hubs that sustain high daily demand for quick-service dining.

States such as Pulau Pinang (23), Perak (18), Pahang (17), Negeri Sembilan (16), and Melaka (12) record moderate outlet counts, reflecting a balanced mix of urbanization, tourism, and local consumer spending power. These areas continue to benefit from steady economic activity and expanding suburban communities that support franchise growth outside the Klang Valley core.

Meanwhile, northern and eastern states like Kedah (10), Terengganu (5), and Kelantan (6) display slower outlet expansion. Their lower density of outlets suggests smaller urban markets, fewer commercial hubs, and less exposure to high-volume retail zones compared to the west coast states. This highlights how population distribution and income level play key roles in shaping fast-food accessibility.

In East Malaysia, Sabah (15) and Sarawak (13) show significantly fewer outlets relative to their population size, while Labuan (1), Perlis (1), and Putrajaya (4) have minimal coverage. These patterns underline ongoing regional inequalities in infrastructure and urban concentration, where McDonald's presence aligns closely with economic development and accessibility across Malaysian states.

Tourism Arrivals vs McDonald's Outlet Count by State (2022)

Understanding Tourism and McDonald's Outlets (2022): States with high tourism arrivals generally have more McDonald's outlets, reflecting demand from both locals and visitors. Selangor (21.99M arrivals, 97 outlets) and Kuala Lumpur (16.91M, 45 outlets) lead the way as major urban and tourist hubs, supported by dense populations and continuous tourist inflows.

Moderate tourism states such as Johor (12.38M, 40 outlets), Pahang (13.19M, 17 outlets), and Pulau Pinang (10.00M, 23 outlets) show proportional outlet counts relative to visitor numbers. These states balance both domestic travel and economic activity, making them sustainable markets for fast food growth.

Low-tourism regions like Perlis (1.67M, 1 outlet), Labuan (0.24M, 1 outlet), and Putrajaya (1.31M, 4 outlets) have limited McDonald's presence, highlighting how low visitor numbers and smaller local populations restrict commercial expansion opportunities.

Overall: Higher tourism typically aligns with more McDonald's outlets, but population density, accessibility, and urban development also play key roles. States with strong transport networks and city centers tend to attract more investment and fast-food chains.

Tourism-driven demand: Popular destinations like Pahang and Pulau Pinang benefit from seasonal surges in visitors, which create steady demand for convenient, recognizable food options like McDonald's. This emphasizes the chain's adaptability to tourism-oriented economies.

Relative Youth Level vs Outlet Count (2022)

The butterfly chart comparing Relative Youth Level and McDonald's Outlet Density highlights interesting patterns across Malaysian states in 2022. States with high youth percentages, such as Sabah (31.2%) and Perlis (31.8%), show relatively low outlet densities (0.22–0.17 outlets per 100,000 people), suggesting that a large youth population alone does not guarantee higher fast food accessibility. This highlights regional disparities, particularly between East and West Malaysia.

Urbanization and economic activity appear to be stronger drivers of McDonald's presence. States like Selangor, despite a lower youth percentage (23.5%), have the highest number of outlets (97) and the highest density (0.69 per 100k), reflecting its status as a highly urbanized and commercially developed state. Similarly, Johor, Pulau Pinang, and Melaka maintain moderate youth populations but comparatively high outlet densities, showing that tourism and economic hubs attract more fast food outlets.

The Normalized Youth metric further emphasizes mismatches between youth population and outlet availability. In states such as Kelantan, Terengganu, and Sabah, youth levels are high but outlet densities remain low, indicating potential untapped markets or barriers to commercial expansion. Conversely, states like Selangor and Melaka exhibit a more balanced relationship, where youth presence and McDonald's accessibility align more closely.

Relative Poverty Level vs Outlet Count (2022)

The 2022 data shows a clear inverse relationship between McDonald's outlet density and relative poverty levels across Malaysian states. Selangor, Pulau Pinang, and Negeri Sembilan have the highest outlet densities, coupled with low normalized poverty rates, highlighting how fast-food chains tend to concentrate in economically prosperous and urbanized regions.

States like Sabah, Kelantan, and Terengganu have lower outlet densities and higher relative poverty levels. Sabah, for instance, exhibits the highest normalized poverty rate (1.0) yet has only 15 outlets relative to its population, reflecting regional disparities in commercial access and economic development.

Mid-tier states such as Pahang, Perak, and Kedah show moderate values in both metrics, suggesting balanced economic conditions and commercial activity. These states represent potential areas for business expansion while also indicating varying levels of socioeconomic development across Malaysia.

Overall, the chart highlights how states with higher economic well-being and urbanization tend to have greater McDonald's outlet density, while regions with higher poverty levels have fewer outlets. This pattern suggests that outlet distribution is influenced by factors such as local purchasing power, population density, and urban infrastructure, reflecting the economic and demographic conditions across Malaysian states.

Median Income vs McDonald's Outlet Density (2022)

The scatter plot examining Median Income and McDonald's Outlet Density reveals a clear positive trend across Malaysian states in 2022. States with higher median incomes, such as Selangor and Pulau Pinang, also show higher outlet densities (1.37 and 1.32 outlets per 100k respectively), indicating that economic prosperity strongly correlates with fast-food accessibility.

Population size adds another layer of influence. Densely populated states like Johor combine both substantial populations and moderate median income (0.99 outlets per 100k), resulting in more outlets compared to smaller states like Perlis, where moderate income alone does not translate into higher outlet density.

The regression line plotted on the chart highlights the overall positive relationship between median income and outlet density. States lying above the line, such as Melaka and Negeri Sembilan, indicate higher outlet density than expected based on income alone, suggesting that factors like urbanization, tourism, or commercial hubs contribute to additional outlets beyond income effects.

Overall, the chart demonstrates that McDonald's outlet density is influenced by a combination of income, population, and urban concentration. States that balance these factors tend to have the highest outlet density, while regions with lower income or smaller populations generally maintain fewer outlets per 100,000 people.
